# Cheatsheet: Quick Reference & Decision Rules

## The 5 Fundamentals Checklist

- [ ] **Attention:** Headline captures; uses specificity, curiosity, or loss aversion; promises reader benefit
- [ ] **Advantage:** Every claim translated to "you/your" benefit; features not emphasized
- [ ] **Prove It:** At least one proof type included (testimonial, expert, test, guarantee, demo)
- [ ] **Convince:** Benefits consolidated; reader can visualize using product
- [ ] **Call to Action:** Explicit, simple, specific; deadline or urgency included; removes obstacles

**Red flag:** Missing even one principle = weak ad.

---

## Headline Quick Test

| Headline Type | Power | Use When | Avoid If |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interrogative (question) | ⭐⭐⭐ High | Want curiosity appeal | Topic obvious/uninteresting |
| Specific numbers | ⭐⭐⭐ High | Have quantifiable benefit | Numbers are generic |
| Benefit promise | ⭐⭐ Medium | Direct, clear benefit needed | Need curiosity/interest |
| Negative consequence | ⭐⭐⭐ High | Loss aversion is motivator | Risk of seeming alarmist |
| Vague generality | ❌ None | Never | Always; always rewrite |

**Decision rule:** Use interrogative or specific numbers when uncertain. Both outperform generic benefits.

## Consumer Motivator Quick Match

**Select the STRONGEST motivator for your product:**

| Motivator | Best For | Copy Angle |
|---|---|---|
| Money/Better Job | Career-focused, mid-income | Earnings, advancement, security |
| Security in Old Age | Risk-averse, older audiences | Independence, worry-free future |
| Popularity | Social, younger audiences | Inclusion, being sought-after, status |
| Praise/Recognition | Achievement-focused | Competence, taste, accomplishment |
| Comfort | Tired, hardworking audiences | Relief, luxury, earned rest |
| Better Appearance | Style-conscious | Pride, attractiveness, self-improvement |
| Personal Prestige | Universal (strongest across demos) | Dignity, avoiding embarrassment, character |

| Leisure | Work-life balance audiences | Entertainment, pleasure, relaxation |
| Social Ascension | Ambitious audiences | Advancement, better circles, improvement |

**Rule:** Pick ONE primary motivator. Don't scatter across multiple appeals.

## Proof Strength Ranking

**Most to least persuasive:**

1. **Demonstration** (product in use) — See benefit directly
2. **Testimonial** (peer similar to reader, geographically close) — Trust from similarity
3. **Expert Endorsement** (recognized authority in field) — Trust from expertise
4. **Test Results** (measured, methodology stated) — Trust from data
5. **Guarantee** (risk removal, clear terms) — Trust from company confidence
6. **Testimonial** (generic, distant) — Some trust from social proof
7. **Reputation** (years in business, scale) — Background trust
8. **Feature list alone** — No proof; facts without emotion fail

**Application:** Lead with strongest available proof. Combine 2-3 proof types for maximum conviction.

## Copy Length Decision at a Glance

**Is inherent appeal strong?** (Product naturally interesting, benefits obvious)
- YES → Short copy possible (50-200 words)
- NO → Long copy needed (1,000+ words)

**Is price high?** ($1,000+)
- YES → Long copy (need extensive proof)
- NO → Medium copy possible

**Is decision complex?** (High involvement, multiple factors)
- YES → Long copy (education needed)
- NO → Short copy possible

**Can you hold interest?** (Do you have multiple strong points?)
- YES → Go as long as needed
- NO → Cut ruthlessly to strongest point

**Rule of thumb:** "As long as needed, never longer." If interest drops, you've gone too long.

## First Paragraph Fails (Red Flags)

- ❌ Doesn't follow headline's promise
- ❌ Uses long, complex sentences (should be brief and punchy)
- ❌ Starts with product features instead of reader benefit
- ❌ Delays promised reward (should be immediate)
- ❌ Fails to maintain interest (reader leaves here)

**Diagnostic:** If you can skip your first paragraph and start with paragraph 2, your first paragraph failed.

---

## Two-Force Rule: Emotional + Proof

**Emotional appeal alone** → Desire without conviction → No sale

**Proof/facts alone** → Conviction without desire → Overthinking, no sale

**Emotional + Proof together** → Desire + conviction → Sale

**Application:** 
1. Open with emotional benefit
2. Support every major claim with proof
3. Close with emotional benefit + proof summary

---

## The Compound Effect Trap (What NOT to Do)

**False belief:** Multiple exposures compound into eventual sales
**Reality:** Zero multiplied by any number = zero

**If a campaign doesn't produce immediate results:**
- Fix the ad first (don't run more weak ads)
- Don't rely on cumulative effect to salvage weak campaign
- One great ad beats ten mediocre ads

**Test:** Does this ad work NOW? If no, fix it before repeating.

## Urgency Stimulants Ranking

| Stimulant | Strength | Duration | Overuse Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time limit (deadline) | ⭐⭐⭐ Very High | Effective | High; must be real |
| Limited stock | ⭐⭐⭐ Very High | Effective | High; must be truthful |
| Price increase threat | ⭐⭐⭐ High | Good | Medium; can feel pushy |
| Losses vs Gains appeal | ⭐⭐⭐ High | Good | Low; psychologically sound |
| Guarantee/risk removal | ⭐⭐ Medium | Lasting | Low; builds trust |
| Free bonus/offer | ⭐⭐ Medium | Good | Medium; devalues over time |

**Combo rule:** Time limit + limited stock + price increase = maximum urgency (but use sparingly).

## Gender-Based Copy Angle Quick Reference

| Audience | Psychology | Copy Focus | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Female | Short-term | Immediate, specific, event-based | "When you're running late, this saves 15 min daily" |
| Male | Long-term | Future, advancement, security | "This skill advances your career over 5 years" |

**Paradox:** Emphasizing specific/immediate actually resonates broadly (both genders), so female-focused copy often wins overall.

## Copy Quality Tells

**Signs your copy is working:**
- ✅ Reader can't skip the first paragraph
- ✅ Emotional connection is clear
- ✅ Every claim has supporting proof
- ✅ Call to action is impossible to miss
- ✅ Reader visualizes using product

**Signs your copy is failing:**
- ❌ Reads like feature list
- ❌ No emotional appeal (just facts)
- ❌ Reader doesn't know what action to take
- ❌ Copy is longer than needed to make sale
- ❌ Focus on product, not reader benefit

---

## The Reader Portrait Checklist

Build this before writing. Know your reader:

- [ ] Age, gender, income level
- [ ] Education and sophistication level
- [ ] Daily concerns and pain points
- [ ] What they already believe (superstitions, habits)
- [ ] What motivates them most (use 10 motivators)
- [ ] What they already know about this category
- [ ] What they're skeptical about
- [ ] Where/when they'll encounter your ad
- [ ] Similar reader they might reference themselves
- [ ] End result they want (not product, but outcome)

**Rule:** Write directly to this portrait. Use "you/your" as if speaking to one person.

---

## Anti-Patterns (Never Do This)

- ❌ Start with vague generality ("Quality products cost less")
- ❌ Use clever wordplay instead of clarity
- ❌ Emphasize features over benefits
- ❌ Try multiple appeals (scatter focus)
- ❌ Assume reader will infer action (state explicitly)
- ❌ Include facts without emotional setup
- ❌ Include emotion without factual support
- ❌ Make call to action vague or implicit
- ❌ Use reader-distancing language ("people" instead of "you")
- ❌ Trust compound effect (one weak ad repeated)

---

## The Copywriter's Doubt Checklist

After drafting, ask yourself:

- [ ] Is my announcement convincing enough to change minds?
- [ ] Is my campaign persuasive enough to create demand?
- [ ] Can my copy overcome weak sales support?
- [ ] Can I neutralize price disadvantages?
- [ ] Would *I* buy this based on this copy?
- [ ] Can reader skip any essential section without losing persuasiveness?
- [ ] Is my strongest benefit leading?
- [ ] Is my call to action unmissable?

**Principle:** Doubt drives better work. Complacency kills it.

## Quick Reference: The 5 Fundamentals in One Ad

```
HEADLINE (Attention):
"How to [specific benefit] in [timeframe]"

FIRST PARAGRAPH (Advantage):
"Most people [obstacle]. But if you [solution], 
you'll [benefit]. Here's why..."

MIDDLE SECTION (Prove It):
[Testimonial + Expert endorsement + Test results]

PERSUASION SECTION (Convince):
Paint vivid picture of reader using product.
Emphasize gains and losses.

CLOSING (Call to Action):
"Call 1-800-XXX today. Offer expires Friday.
Only 50 slots available. Risk-free 30-day guarantee."
```

## Decision Flowchart: "Should I Use Longer Copy?"

```
START
  ↓
Is interest high throughout? → NO → Make copy shorter
  ↓ YES
Is the appeal inherent & strong? → YES → Short copy works
  ↓ NO
Is price high ($500+)? → YES → Longer copy needed
  ↓ NO
Is decision complex? → YES → Longer copy needed
  ↓ NO
Do you have multiple proof points? → YES → Can be longer
  ↓ NO
END: Use just long enough to complete sale work.
Never longer. Density wins.
```
