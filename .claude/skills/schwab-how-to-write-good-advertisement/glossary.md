# Glossary: Schwab Copywriting Terms

**5 Fundamentals** — Schwab's core framework: Get Attention → Show Advantage → Prove It → Convince → Call to Action. Every effective ad follows this structure. (Ch 1-5)

**A/B Testing** — Testing two ad variations where only one element differs; only count differences >15% significant; "shout not whisper." (Ch 11)

**Advantage (Show Advantage)** — Second principle: translate product features into reader benefits using "you/your" language and reader-centric positioning. (Ch 2)

**Attention (Get Attention)** — First principle: headline and opening that stops reader; >50% of ad value lives here; use curiosity, specificity, loss aversion. (Ch 1)

**Call to Action** — Fifth principle: explicit request for reader to take action; must remove obstacles and make action simple, specific, immediate. (Ch 5)

**Compound Effect (The Compound Effect Fallacy)** — Mistaken belief that multiple weak exposures accumulate into eventual sales; in reality, zero multiplied by any number = zero. (Ch 12)

**Consumer Motivators (10)** — Health, Comfort, Money, Leisure, Popularity, Pride, Appearance, Business Success, Security, Status. These are the only human desires that drive purchase decisions. (Ch 2)

**Conviction** — Emotional belief created through emotional appeals and factual support working together; emotion alone creates desire, facts alone create doubt. (Ch 3)

**Copy Length** — Determined by product type, price, reader sophistication, and inherent appeal strength; interest is the only variable that matters. (Ch 6)

**Copywriter Mindset (Posture Durona)** — Three principles: sales-first (not compliments), know red flags (what repeatedly fails), develop humility through doubt. (Ch 13)

**Decision Tree** — Step-by-step framework for making copy choices (e.g., copy length decision matrix based on product type and appeal strength). (Ch 6)

**Desire** — Reader's emotional pull toward product, created through emotional appeals and reader-centric benefits; must be supported by proof to create conviction. (Ch 3)

**Doubt (Habit of Doubt)** — Symptom of copywriter maturity; questioning every claim drives better work; complacency is the enemy of effective copy. (Ch 13)

**Emotional Appeal** — The first force in sales (emotion + reason = conversion); awakens desire, makes reader want product; necessary but insufficient without proof. (Ch 2, 3)

**Fact (Types of)** — Reputation, prestige, production, quality, delivery speed, product results, demonstrations, testimonials, expert testimonials, awards/contests. (Ch 3)

**First Paragraph Rule** — Opening paragraph determines whether reader continues; must follow headline promise with brief sentences and immediate benefit delivery. (Ch 2)

**Gender Messaging** — Male psychology emphasizes long-term (career, future security); female psychology emphasizes short-term (immediate, specific, event-based). (Ch 9)

**Guarantee** — Proof technique that eliminates reader's risk and builds conviction; should be generous and clear; combine with promises for stronger effect. (Ch 5)

**Headline** — Opening that captures attention and promises reward; >50% of ad value; use interrogative, specific numbers, curiosity, or negative consequences. (Ch 1)

**Interest** — The single variable that determines copy effectiveness; longer copy wins if it holds interest; short copy wins only if it holds interest. (Ch 6)

**Loss Aversion** — Psychological principle: fear of loss is stronger than desire for gain; appeals to "what you'll miss if you don't act" outperform gain-focused appeals. (Ch 14)

**Mental Model (Reader Portrait)** — Understanding reader's education, skills, beliefs, superstitions, daily concerns; copywriter writes to this portrait directly. (Ch 2)

**New Psychology** — Studies what people actually do when exposed to advertisements, not what they think about ads; outcome-focused, not thought-focused. (Ch 13)

**Old Psychology** — Studies what people think about ads; focuses on attention and interest; outcome-agnostic. (Ch 13)

**Posture Durona** — Italian phrase meaning the professional stance of a skilled copywriter; hardened by experience, focused on sales results, driven by doubt. (Ch 13)

**Practical Illustration** — Worked example of ad structure demonstrating all five principles in a single campaign (e.g., fire prevention device). (Ch 4)

**Proof (Prove It)** — Third principle: factual evidence that supports emotional claims; makes reader feel safe and smart buying; builds conviction. (Ch 3)

**Reader-Centric** — Copy philosophy: every claim translated to reader benefit using "you/your"; benefits not features; reader perspective not product perspective. (Ch 1-5)

**Recency/Primacy** — Learning principle: points at beginning or end of message are more remembered than middle; structure accordingly. (Ch 14)

**Substantive Proof** — Emotional appeal that reminds reader of positive memories (nostalgia marketing); works well for established products. (Ch 11)

**Testimonial** — Peer recommendation; most powerful when geographically close to reader or from person reader resembles. (Ch 3)

**Two-Force Rule** — Emotional appeal + factual proof = conversion; emotional appeal alone or facts alone are insufficient; both necessary. (Ch 3)

**Urgency Stimulants** — Deadline (limit time), scarcity (limited stock), price rise threat, losses & gains, guarantee/risk removal. (Ch 5)

**Value Perception** — Advertising's core job: build brilliant perception of value in reader's mind such that value overshadows price element. (Ch 4)

**Vague Generalities (Anti-pattern)** — Weak headlines/copy that fail to specify: "quality products" vs "saves 30 minutes daily"; specificity required. (Ch 1, 6)
