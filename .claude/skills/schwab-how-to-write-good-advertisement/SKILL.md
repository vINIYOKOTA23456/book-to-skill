# Victor Schwab: How to Write a Good Advertisement

Master copywriting from one of advertising's founding practitioners. Schwab's frameworks remain the foundation of effective sales copy.

---

## The 5 Fundamentals Framework

All effective ads follow this exact structure. **Every principle is necessary.**

### 1. Get Attention
Your headline and opening must stop the reader. >50% of ad value lives here. Use psychological triggers: curiosity, loss aversion, specificity, or interrogative structure.

**When to use:** Always. This is non-negotiable. No attention = no read.

**How:** Specific benefits beat generalities. Use "you/your". Create pattern interrupts. Quantify. Use questions.

### 2. Show Advantage
Translate features into reader benefits. Not "what it is" — "what it does for *you*".

**The reader-centric rule:** Connect every claim to reader's desires, not product attributes.

### 3. Prove It
Emotion creates desire; facts justify the decision. Facts build conviction. They give readers something to tell themselves and others.

**Proof types (ranked by power):**
- Testimonials (especially from people like your reader, geographically close)
- Expert endorsements (recognized authority)
- Demonstrations (show in use, not inert product)
- Tests and results (performance, guarantees)
- Company reputation and prestige
- Quality certifications

**The two-force rule:** Emotional appeal + factual proof = sale. Emotion alone = wasted effort. Facts alone = unconvincing.

### 4. Convince People to Take Advantage
Consolidate benefits into urgent, irresistible offer. Paint vivid picture of reader using product. Emphasize what they *gain* and what they *lose* if they don't act.

### 5. Call to Action
Explicit request for action. Must ask. Readers don't assume. Remove obstacles. Make action simple, specific, immediate.

**Action stimulants:**
- Make an offer (free trial, sample, booklet, bonus)
- Limit the time (deadline creates urgency)
- Limited stock (scarcity appeal)
- Price rising (threat of loss)
- Losses & Gains (what you'll miss if you don't act now)
- Guarantees (eliminate risk, ensure satisfaction)

## The 10 Consumer Motivators

These are the only desires that move readers to action. Every ad targets one or more.

**Money & Better Job** — Financial security, career advancement, entrepreneurship. Strongest for middle-income, career-focused audiences.

**Security in Old Age** — Freedom from worry, independence, peace of mind. Appeal to prudence and future planning.

**Popularity** — Desire to be included, sought-after, socially recognized. Tap into belonging and status.

**Praise & Recognition** — Acknowledgment of competence and taste. Appeal to achievement and self-respect.

**More Comfort** — Relief from work, luxury, leisure, social ascension. Reward after hard work.

**Better Appearance** — Physical attractiveness, self-improvement. Appeal to pride and personal standards.

**Personal Prestige** — Dignity, avoiding embarrassment, maintaining character. Strongest appeal across demographics.

**Health & Energy** — Vigor, vitality, recovery, avoiding decline. Emphasize capability and feeling good.

**Leisure** — Entertainment, pleasure, enjoyment. Balance with earned rest narrative.

**Social Ascension** — Moving up social circles, associating with better people. Paradoxically, make it specific to feel general.

## Headline Strategy: >50% of Your Ad's Power

Your headline must:
- Promise a reward (benefit, curiosity, or negative consequence)
- Stop the reader (pattern interrupt, specificity, numbers)
- Use "you/your" language

**Headline Types That Work:**
- Interrogative (question) — awakens curiosity
- Specific claim (with numbers) — "Save 30 minutes daily"
- Curiosity hook — hints at solution
- Negative consequence — "Don't make this mistake"
- Reader-direct address — "You already know this, but..."

**What kills headlines:**
- Vague generalities ("Quality products")
- Clever wordplay (confuses reader)
- Emphasis on product features, not benefits
- Jargon or unfamiliar terms

## The Three-Stage Sales Process

**Stage 1: Headline catches attention**
→ Reader stops scrolling

**Stage 2: First paragraph catches interest** 
→ Reader decides to continue reading

- Follow the headline's promise immediately
- Use short, clear sentences
- Start with strongest benefit
- Condense biggest promises into opening
- Ask a pertinent question to pull reader forward

**Stage 3: Body copy maintains interest and convinces**
→ Reader believes and wants to act

- Connect every claim to reader's needs
- Stick to purchase points (advantages, not features)
- One main sales angle (don't fragment with multiple appeals)
- Dramatize facts, paint mental images
- Use present tense, singular ("you" not "people")
- Keep paragraphs short
- Use subheadings with novelty/curiosity appeals
- Make text naturally fun and engaging

## Copy Length Decision Matrix

**Length depends on:**

1. **Product type** (impulse buy vs complex decision)
2. **Product price** (higher price = longer copy needed)
3. **Your objective** (immediate action vs awareness)
4. **Inherent appeal strength** (strongest single factor)
   - Strong appeal = short copy works
   - Weak appeal = longer copy needed to overcome
5. **Reader sophistication** (simple vs complex purchase)

**The cardinal rule:** Longer copy wins if it holds interest. Interest is the keyword. The more you maintain reader interest, the higher your conversion.

**Key principle:** Every additional word must contribute. No padding. Copy is hired help to perform specific work — make it long enough to finish the job, never longer.

## Gender-Based Messaging Strategy

**Male psychology ("Long-Term Thinking"):**
Focus on future: career development, children's education, life insurance, security in retirement, professional advancement.

**Female psychology ("Short-Term Thinking"):**
Focus on immediate: specific events, relationships, immediate needs, personal circumstances, specific benefits.

**Application:** For female audiences, emphasize immediate/imminent/specific benefits (paradoxically, specific is general enough to interest all demographics).

## Facts vs Fantasies: The Dream Sell

Sell the dream, not the thing.
- Sell homes, not houses
- Sell beauty, not cosmetics  
- Sell comfort, not furniture
- Sell the transformation, not the product

**Strategy:** Balance romantic connotation with factual support. Highlight glamour and dreams; leave facts in background as proof. Excess facts hinder imagination.

**When facts alone fail:** An advertisement filled only with mundane specifications kills the appeal. Open new horizons with the emotional story; use facts as foundation, not focus.

## Copywriter Mindset: Posture Durona

Three principles define successful copywriters:

**1. Sales First, Compliments Never**
Copy exists to generate sales and profits, never for client approval. A campaign is good only when it proves itself through orders and revenue.

**2. Know What to Avoid (Red Flags)**
As important as knowing what works: recognize what repeatedly fails. Learn to spot the dangers:
- Campaigns without immediate results rarely succeed
- Don't rely on "compound effect" (cumulative impressions rarely close sales)
- Overly complex decisions need longer copy, but "too long" still loses attention
- Traditional/established approaches are not automatically effective

**3. Develop Humility Through Doubt**
Doubt drives better work. Question everything:
- "Is my announcement convincing enough to change minds?"
- "Is my campaign persuasive enough to create demand?"
- "Can my copy overcome weak sales support?"
- "Can I neutralize price disadvantages?"

**Complacency is the enemy.** The less skilled overestimate themselves; skilled practitioners underestimate and dig deeper.

## The Compound Effect Fallacy

**Do NOT assume multiple small exposures compound into eventual action.**

A campaign that fails to create immediate impact usually won't accumulate effect over time. Zero multiplied by any number remains zero.

**Principle:** For a campaign to be worth running, it must do all its sales work in a single time — from headline to call to action — without disregard for efforts made at each stage and without leaving any work unfinished.

Only after proving immediate effectiveness can you consider cumulative benefit a bonus.

## Supporting Frameworks

### The First Paragraph Rule
The first paragraph determines if reader continues. It must:
1. Follow the headline's promise
2. Use brief, easy sentences
3. Start with strongest benefit
4. Fulfill the reading reward promised in headline

### The Reader Portrait
Understand your reader's psychology:
- Education, skills, characteristics
- Daily concerns and feelings
- Personal beliefs and superstitions
- What "ordinary people" believe affects your reader

**Horace's principle:** If you want your audience to cry, you must cry first. Understand your reader's mindset so thoroughly that you can represent their perspective authentically.

### Decision Rule: Interest > Length
Long copy wins. Short copy loses. *Unless* the copy cannot hold interest. Interest is the only variable that matters.

---

## Common Pitfalls

**Pitfall: Trusting "Compound Effect"**
Don't expect repeated weak ads to eventually work. One strong ad outperforms many weak ones.

**Pitfall: Ignoring the First Paragraph**
Many ads fail here. Readers decide to keep reading or leave entirely based on paragraph one.

**Pitfall: Too Many Options**
"Choose from 50 styles and colors" paralyzes buyers. Limit choices to 2-3 strong options.

**Pitfall: Unclear Call to Action**
Readers won't infer what you want. Explicitly ask for the specific action: "Call now", "Order today", "Reply within 10 days".

**Pitfall: Facts Without Emotion**
Proof without emotional appeal fails. Emotion without proof fails. Both are necessary.
