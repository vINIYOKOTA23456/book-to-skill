# Cheatsheet: Quick Reference & Decision Rules

## The 28-Step System At A Glance

**Research & Positioning (Steps 1-3):**
1. Get "Into" the Customer (10 Smart Questions)
2. Get "Into" the Offer (Features → Benefits)
3. Create Damaging Admission & Address Flaws

**Physical Delivery (Steps 4-6):**
4. Get Your Sales Letter Delivered (Envelope strategy)
5. Get Your Sales Letter Looked At (Open envelope)
6. Get Your Sales Letter Read (Headline + first paragraph)

**Copy Execution (Steps 7-28):**
7. Beat the Price Bugaboo (7 price minimizers)
8. Review Winning Copywriting Techniques
9-11. Write First Draft → Rewrite for Strategy → Rewrite for Style
12-14. Answer Questions & Objections → Spark Immediate Action → Creative P.S.
15-27. Check Checklists → Graphic Enhancement → Edit for Passion/Clarity → Compare to Examples → Pretest → Mock-ups → Cool Off → Second Opinions → Final Review
28. Mail!

## The 10 Smart Questions Quick Reference

| # | Question | Listening For | Action |
|---|----------|---------------|--------|
| 1 | What keeps them awake at night? | Anxiety, fears, concerns | Address these fears in copy |
| 2 | What are they afraid of? | Loss scenarios, embarrassment | Tap loss aversion psychology |
| 3 | What are they angry about? | Injustice, being wronged | Channel anger into motivation |
| 4 | What are their top 3 frustrations? | Daily pain points | Solve specific frustrations |
| 5 | What trends are occurring? | Industry shifts, threats | Position offer as adaptation |
| 6 | What do they secretly desire? | Hidden wants, status goals | Emphasize secret benefit |
| 7 | Is there a decision bias? | Analytical? Emotional? Peer-driven? | Match your copy to bias |
| 8 | Do they have their own language? | Industry jargon, terminology | Speak their vernacular |
| 9 | Who else is selling similarly? | Competitor offers, positioning | Differentiate vs. competition |
| 10 | Who has tried and failed? | Previous failed attempts | Learn from failures, avoid them |

## Headline Effectiveness Matrix

| Headline Type | Emotional Power | Curiosity | Urgency | Best For | Avoid If |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| They Didn't Think __ But __ | ⭐⭐⭐ High | ⭐⭐ Medium | ⭐⭐ Medium | Underdog appeal, transformation | Topic obvious/uninteresting |
| Who Else Wants __ | ⭐⭐ Medium | ⭐⭐⭐ High | ⭐⭐ Medium | Social proof appeal, curiosity | Crowded market (feels generic) |
| How __ Made Me __ | ⭐⭐⭐ High | ⭐⭐⭐ High | ⭐⭐ Medium | Story-based offers, transformation | Complex products |
| Are You __ | ⭐⭐⭐ High | ⭐⭐ Medium | ⭐⭐⭐ High | Direct engagement, problem-solution | Professional/formal audiences |
| How To __ | ⭐⭐ Medium | ⭐ Low | ⭐⭐ Medium | Clear benefit, how-to products | Need curiosity/intrigue |
| Give Me __ & I'll __ | ⭐⭐ Medium | ⭐⭐ Medium | ⭐⭐⭐ High | Simple offers, time/money savings | Complex products |
| Secrets of __ | ⭐⭐ Medium | ⭐⭐⭐ High | ⭐ Low | Insider knowledge, premium positioning | Risk of over-promising |
| WARNING: __ | ⭐⭐⭐ High | ⭐⭐ Medium | ⭐⭐⭐ High | Problem-solution copy, fear-based | Professional/formal audiences |

**Decision Rule:** Use "How To," "Are You," or specific-number variations when uncertain. These outperform generic benefit headlines.

## The Three Master Formulas Decision Tree

```
START: What's your primary motivation for reader?

├─ AVOID PAIN? (Most powerful)
│  └─ Use: PROBLEM-AGITATION-SOLUTION
│     Structure: Define problem → Agitate emotions → Unveil solution
│     Best for: Complex objections, high-stakes decisions, B2B
│
├─ FASCINATED BY FUTURE?
│  └─ Use: FORTUNE TELLING
│     Structure: What's coming? Predict trends, opportunities, threats
│     Best for: Newsletter subscriptions, premium positioning, trend-based
│
└─ FEAR OF BEING LEFT BEHIND?
   └─ Use: WINNERS & LOSERS
      Structure: Stark contrast (2 groups, 2 outcomes)
      Best for: Competitive markets, aspiration-based, all demographics
```

## Price Minimizer Selection Chart

| Price Challenge | Solution #1 | Solution #2 | Solution #3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price seems too high | Apples-to-oranges comparison | Bulk value breakdown | Development cost story |
| Sticker shock | Monthly installments | Payment plans | Hidden benefit emphasis |
| Complex/bundled offer | Component value breakdown | Apples-to-oranges to alternative | Bulk emphasis + installments |
| Information products | Bulk value (weight, size, count) | Apples-to-oranges to courses/seminars | List of contained items |
| High-ticket ($500+) | Component breakdown + guarantee | Development cost + testimonials | Monthly payments + guarantee |
| Time-intensive product | Hidden benefit (time saved/freedom) | Bulk value + efficiency story | Apples-to-oranges (time cost) |

**Rule:** Always use at least 2 minimizers for high-ticket offers.

## Copy Length Decision Matrix

| Question | Answer YES | Answer NO |
|---|---|---|
| **Is inherent appeal strong?** | Short copy (50-200 words) | Longer copy (1,000+ words) |
| **Is price $500+?** | Longer copy (extensive proof) | Medium/shorter possible |
| **Is decision complex?** | Longer copy (education) | Shorter possible |
| **Can you hold interest?** | As long as needed | Cut ruthlessly to strongest |
| **Multiple proof points available?** | Can be longer | Keep concise |

**Final Rule:** "As long as needed, never longer." Length = amount of sales work required. Interest is the only variable that matters.

## First Paragraph Red Flags (Diagnostic)

**If your first paragraph has ANY of these, rewrite:**

- ❌ Doesn't follow headline's promise (reader confused)
- ❌ Long, complex sentences (loses busy reader immediately)
- ❌ Starts with product features instead of benefit (wrong angle)
- ❌ Delays promised reward (reader stops reading)
- ❌ No clear emotional connection (feels cold)
- ❌ Too long (more than 3-4 sentences)

**Quick Test:** Can reader skip first paragraph and start with paragraph 2 without losing persuasiveness? If YES, first paragraph failed.

## Copywriting Technique Quick Match

| Situation | Best Technique | Runner-Up |
|---|---|---|
| Reader skeptical/cynical | Damaging Admission | Testimonial (from similar person) |
| Reader doesn't understand benefit | Case History (before/after) | Testimonial with specific results |
| Price is main objection | Apples-to-oranges comparison | Monthly installments |
| Reader fears making wrong choice | Guarantee/Risk Removal | Expert endorsement |
| Reader needs social proof | Testimonial (similar, geographically close) | "Most will buy" positioning |
| Intangible benefit needs tangibility | Slice-of-life story, Case history | Before/after visuals |
| Reader distrusts offer | Expert endorsement | Substantive proof (facts/tests) |

## Psychological Principle Quick Reference

| Principle | How It Works | Copywriting Application |
|---|---|---|
| Loss Aversion | Fear of loss > desire for gain | Use Problem-Agitation-Solution formula |
| Social Proof | Others want it → I should too | "Thousands have joined..." / Testimonials |
| Scarcity | Limited supply = higher value | "Only 50 slots available" / "While supplies last" |
| Authority | Expert recommendation = credible | Expert testimonials, credentials |
| Reciprocity | Free gift = obligation to respond | Free reports, samples, consultations |
| Liking | Similar people = more persuasive | Testimonials from people like reader |
| Urgency | Time pressure = quick decision | Deadlines, price increases, limited availability |

**Master Principle:** Emotion (desire/fear) + Proof (facts/evidence) = Sale. Either alone fails.

## Tells That Your Copy Is Working

- ✅ Reader can't skip first paragraph
- ✅ Emotional connection is clear and immediate
- ✅ Every major claim has proof/support
- ✅ Call to action is impossible to miss
- ✅ Reader visualizes using product/service
- ✅ Objections are addressed before reader thinks of them
- ✅ Price feels justified by value presented
- ✅ Reader feels urgency to act NOW, not "someday"

---

## Tells That Your Copy Is Failing

- ❌ Reads like feature list (not benefits)
- ❌ No emotional appeal, just facts
- ❌ Reader doesn't know what action to take
- ❌ Longer than needed to make the sale
- ❌ Focus on product, not reader benefit
- ❌ No proof/support for major claims
- ❌ Vague headlines or generic benefits
- ❌ Reader has multiple interpretations of what to do

## The Professional Copywriter Doubt Checklist

After drafting, ask yourself:

- [ ] Is my headline convincing enough to change minds?
- [ ] Is my copy persuasive enough to create demand?
- [ ] Can my copy overcome weak sales support?
- [ ] Can I neutralize price disadvantages?
- [ ] Would *I* buy based on this copy?
- [ ] Can reader skip any section without losing persuasiveness?
- [ ] Is my strongest benefit leading?
- [ ] Is my call to action unmissable?
- [ ] Have I addressed the top 3 reader objections?
- [ ] Does first paragraph follow headline promise?

**Principle:** Doubt drives better work. Complacency kills it.

## Quick Problem-Agitation-Solution Template

```
PROBLEM:
[State clearly, factually. Aim for reader agreement.]

AGITATION:
[Stir up emotion. Tap fear/anger/resentment. Paint consequences vividly.]

SOLUTION:
[Position your offer as answer. Reader mentally ready.]

RESULT:
Reader says: "I've got to do something about this! Thank goodness you have the answer!"
```

## The Reader Portrait Checklist

Build BEFORE writing. Know your reader:

- [ ] Age, gender, income level
- [ ] Education and sophistication level  
- [ ] Daily concerns and pain points (top 3)
- [ ] What they already believe (superstitions, habits)
- [ ] What motivates them most (money? status? security?)
- [ ] What they already know about this category
- [ ] What they're skeptical/cynical about
- [ ] Where/when they'll encounter your letter
- [ ] Similar person they might reference themselves
- [ ] End result they want (not product, outcome)

**Rule:** Write directly to this portrait. Use "you/your" as if speaking to one person.

## Anti-Patterns (Never Do This)

- ❌ Start with vague generality ("Quality is our priority")
- ❌ Use clever wordplay instead of clarity
- ❌ Emphasize features over benefits
- ❌ Try multiple competing appeals (scatter focus)
- ❌ Assume reader will infer what you want (state explicitly)
- ❌ Include facts without emotional setup
- ❌ Include emotion without factual support
- ❌ Make call to action vague or implicit
- ❌ Use reader-distancing language ("people" vs "you")
- ❌ Trust compound effect (one weak ad repeated = zero)

## Visualization Preparation Checklist

Before writing, visualize your prospect:

- [ ] See them as a real person (give them a name, face)
- [ ] Visualize their morning: commute, email, coffee
- [ ] Picture when/where they get your mail
- [ ] Picture them standing over wastebasket, sorting mail
- [ ] What's preoccupying them at that moment?
- [ ] What would make them stop sorting and read?
- [ ] What objections would they think of?
- [ ] What proof would convince them?
- [ ] What fears would prevent them from acting?
- [ ] What would make them feel like you understand them?

**Result:** Copy that anticipates every reader thought/objection before they occur.

## The Seven Price Minimizers Ranked by Power

1. **Component Value Breakdown** — Most effective for bundled offers; list each piece separately with "if bought alone" value
2. **Apples-to-Oranges Comparison** — Most effective for information products; compare to much higher-priced alternative
3. **Bulk Value Emphasis** — Most effective for tangible/countable items; emphasize weight, size, quantity, count
4. **Development Cost Story** — Most effective for high-tech/engineered products; reveal R&D investment
5. **Hidden Benefit Emphasis** — Most effective for aspirational offers; emphasize lifestyle/time/status gain
6. **Monthly Installment Concealment** — Most effective for high sticker shock; spread payment psychologically
7. **Payment Plans** — Most effective for accessibility; lower monthly payment barrier

**Rule:** Combine at least 2 for high-ticket offers.
