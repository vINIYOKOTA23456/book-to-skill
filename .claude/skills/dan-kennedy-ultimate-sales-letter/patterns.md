# Patterns: Copywriting Techniques from Dan Kennedy

## Headline Patterns (Fill-in-the-Blank Templates)

### Pattern: They Didn't Think I Could __, But I Did
**When to use:** When underdog appeal resonates with audience; overcoming obstacles narrative.

**How:** Lead with others' doubt/ridicule, then reveal success despite it.

**Examples:**
- "They Laughed When I Sat Down at the Piano—But Not When I Started to Play!"
- "They Grinned When the Waiter Spoke to Me in French—But Their Laughter Changed to Amazement at My Reply!"

**Trade-offs:** High emotional appeal; requires that reader identifies with the "underdog"; works best for personal transformation/skill-building offers.

---

### Pattern: Who Else Wants __?
**When to use:** When you want to imply social proof; others know something reader doesn't.

**How:** Open with question implying crowd of people already want this thing.

**Examples:**
- "Who Else Wants a Screen-Star Figure?"
- "Who Else Needs an Extra Hour Every Day?"

**Trade-offs:** Social proof motivation; subtle scarcity; can feel less urgent than other patterns.

---

### Pattern: How __ Made Me __
**When to use:** First-person story with dramatic contrast/transformation.

**How:** Describe event or discovery, then reveal the impact on author.

**Examples:**
- "How a 'Fool Stunt' Made Me a Star Salesman"
- "How Relocating to Tennessee Saved Our Company One Million Dollars a Year"

**Trade-offs:** Story engagement + concrete benefit; reader must identify with the starting situation.

---

### Pattern: Are You __?
**When to use:** Question format that challenges, provokes, or arouses curiosity.

**How:** Ask provocative question that makes reader nod yes.

**Examples:**
- "Are You Ashamed of the Smells in Your House?"
- "Are You Prepared for the Japanese Invasion of Your Industry?"

**Trade-offs:** Direct engagement; can feel pushy if poorly worded; lower urgency than other patterns.

---

### Pattern: How To __
**When to use:** Straightforward benefit delivery. "How to" = two most powerful words in copywriting.

**How:** State direct benefit in "how to" format.

**Examples:**
- "How to Collect Social Security at Any Age"
- "How to Improve Telemarketers' Productivity for $19.95"

**Variation — Add Flag:** Segment audience first. "For Busy Doctors: How to Educate New Patients in Half the Time"

**Trade-offs:** Clear and direct; works for any product; lower curiosity appeal than question format.

---

### Pattern: Give Me __ and I'll __
**When to use:** When offer is simple and you want to telegraph it clearly.

**How:** State the small commitment required, then state the result delivered.

**Examples:**
- "Give Me 5 Days and I'll Give You a Magnetic Personality"
- "Give Me Just 1 Hour a Day and I'll Have You Speaking French like 'Pierre' in One Month"

**Trade-offs:** Simple promise; reduces reader hesitation; can feel transactional rather than aspirational.

---

## Advanced Headline Patterns

### Pattern: Secrets of __
**When to use:** "Secrets" is powerful word; positions you as insider revealing insider knowledge.

**Examples:**
- "Secrets of a Madison Ave. Maverick—'Contrarian Advertising'"
- "Secrets of Four Champion Golfers"

**Trade-offs:** High curiosity; implies exclusive knowledge; can backfire if promise not delivered.

---

### Pattern: WARNING: __
**When to use:** Problem-solution copy theme; grabs attention with threat.

**Examples:**
- "Warning: Two-Thirds of Middle Managers in Your Industry Will Lose Jobs in 36 Months"
- "Warning: Your 'Corporate Shield' May Be Made of Tissue Paper"

**Trade-offs:** Extremely high attention; can feel alarmist; must follow with credible solution.

---

### Pattern: __ Ways to __
**When to use:** "How to" headline enhanced with intriguing specific number.

**Examples:**
- "101 Ways to Increase New Patient Flow"
- "17 Ways to Slash Your Equipment Maintenance Costs"

**Trade-offs:** Specificity creates credibility; number seems comprehensive; works for list-based offers.

---

## First Paragraph Patterns

### Pattern: Immediate Benefit Bridge
**Structure:** Start by following headline promise; immediately deliver first major benefit in 2-3 short sentences.

**Example:**
- Headline: "How to Write Better Ads in 7 Days"
- First Paragraph: "Most copywriters struggle for months. But if you follow this exact system, you'll write your best ad by Friday. Here's why this works so fast..."

**Trade-offs:** Keeps interest high; can feel rushed if not carefully paced; delivers on headline promise immediately.

---

### Pattern: Curiosity Question Opening
**Structure:** Begin with pertinent question that makes reader want the answer.

**Example:** "Did you know that 70% of ads fail at the headline stage? But there's a proven pattern that changes those odds..."

**Trade-offs:** Engages reader curiosity; requires strong answer to avoid disappointment; pulls reader forward.

---

### Pattern: Problem Statement Agreement
**Structure:** State what reader already knows/believes, then reframe.

**Example:** "We all know that 'quality' doesn't mean much anymore. But there's one specific quality that actually moves customers to buy. It's not what you think..."

**Trade-offs:** Validates reader knowledge; positions you as insider; creates curiosity about the reframe.

## Price Objection Patterns

### Pattern: Apples-to-Oranges Price Comparison
**When to use:** When direct product comparison makes your price look high.

**How:** Compare to higher-priced alternative (seminar, consulting, books, etc.).

**Example:**
- "To attend the Practice Promotion Seminar costs $195 enrollment + travel + lodging = $500+. But via cassette at your convenience with staff, you pay only $95."

**Trade-offs:** Effective for information/course offers; requires finding higher-priced alternative; confuses price issue in reader's mind.

---

### Pattern: Bulk Value Emphasis
**When to use:** Selling information products (books, courses, subscriptions, kits).

**How:** Emphasize weight, size, number of components, quantity of information contained.

**Examples:**
- "Five-Foot Shelf of Books"
- "Over 13 Pounds of Books and Materials"
- "24 Newspaper Ads + 24 Newsletters + 24 Referral Campaigns + 24 Hand-Outs"

**Trade-offs:** Conveys high value; appeals to psychological connection between bulk and worth; works best for tangible/countable items.

---

### Pattern: Development Cost Story
**When to use:** Justify price by revealing research investment.

**How:** Describe brain trust, prototype testing, lab hours, expert time invested.

**Example:**
- "Eight robotics engineers, six-month lab testing, $1M+ R&D investment, 15,000 test repetitions before production."

**Trade-offs:** Builds value perception; makes price relevant; can feel like padding if overused.

---

### Pattern: Component Value Breakdown
**When to use:** Complex offer with multiple components (seminars, kits, ongoing services).

**How:** List each component separately with individual "if bought separately" value, then show total value >> actual price.

**Example:**
- 6 seminars × $395 = $2,370 value
- 24-month marketing kit = $10,000 value  
- Tape-of-month club = $1,000 value
- **Total value = $13,370, but your price = $3,895**

**Trade-offs:** Most powerful price minimizer; requires genuine breakable components; can feel like inflated value if exaggerated.

---

### Pattern: Monthly Installment Concealment
**When to use:** High-ticket offers; payment dread objection.

**How:** State as monthly payment rather than total price.

**Example:** "Just three small monthly installments of $11.95" instead of "$35.85 total"

**Trade-offs:** Psychologically reduces sticker shock; requires installment infrastructure; can backfire if felt manipulative.

## Proof Patterns

### Pattern: Specific Testimonial (From Similar Person)
**When to use:** You have customer matching reader demographic.

**How:** Include name, location, specific result with numbers/time.

**Example:** "As a busy mom in Portland, I thought I'd never find time for this. Then I discovered X and now I spend 2 hours less per week on this task." — Sarah M., Portland OR

**Trade-offs:** Highest credibility when person is similar to reader and geographically close; loses power if generic or distant.

---

### Pattern: Case History (Before/After)
**When to use:** Intangible benefits need to be made tangible.

**How:** Show real person/business in before state, steps taken, after state with specific results.

**Example:** "John's dental practice had 15 new patients monthly. After implementing System X, new patients jumped to 47 monthly. Revenue increased $150K annually."

**Trade-offs:** Very persuasive; shows real-world application; requires permission to use name/details.

---

### Pattern: Expert Endorsement
**When to use:** Authority credibility needed; reader skeptical.

**How:** Name recognized expert with credentials, their endorsement statement.

**Example:** "Dr. James Peterson, leading cardiologist at Johns Hopkins, recommends this approach for..."

**Trade-offs:** Builds authority; requires actual expert permission; only works if expert is recognized by reader.

---

### Pattern: Guarantee/Risk Removal
**When to use:** Reader hesitation is uncertainty or financial risk.

**How:** Offer specific guarantee with clear timeframe and terms.

**Example:** "Try it risk-free for 30 days. If you don't see improvement, we refund every penny. No questions. No hassle."

**Trade-offs:** Removes buying friction; signals confidence; requires honoring guarantee consistently.

---

## Damaging Admission Pattern

**Structure:** Acknowledge specific flaw or limitation of your offer openly.

**Example:**
"If you want waiters in tuxedos with white linen cloths, menus with unpronounceable words, and high-priced wines in silver ice buckets, our restaurant is not for you. But if you want good, solid, home-cooked pasta with real vegetables and spices by real Italian Mama, and you'll trade white linen for red-checked plastic tablecloths, you'll love our place."

**Trade-offs:** Enhances credibility dramatically; addresses objections preemptively; turns competitive disadvantage into selling story.

---

## Call-to-Action Patterns

### Pattern: Clear Direction (Specific Action)
**Structure:** Remove all ambiguity from desired action. Make it simple, specific, immediate.

**Example:** "Call 1-800-BENEFIT right now. Have your credit card ready. Allow 3 minutes."

**Not:** "Let us know if you're interested."

**Trade-offs:** Dramatically increases conversion; requires specific response mechanism; no wishy-washy language.

---

### Pattern: Urgency Trio (Limited Quantity + Time Limit + Price Increase)
**Structure:** Combine three urgency stimulants.

**Example:** "Order by Friday (time limit). Only 100 units available (scarcity). Price increases $50 next week (cost)."

**Trade-offs:** Maximizes urgency; can feel manipulative if overused; must be truthful.

---

### Pattern: Risk Removal + Incentive Combo
**Structure:** Eliminate hesitation (guarantee) + add incentive (bonus) together.

**Example:** "Order today and receive free 30-day trial course ($197 value) + our 60-day money-back guarantee. You risk nothing."

**Trade-offs:** Most persuasive combo; requires genuine incentive; emphasize risk removal first, then bonus.

---

## Problem-Agitation-Solution Application Pattern

**Step 1 — Problem Definition:** State problem clearly, factually. Aim for reader agreement. Can be known or unknown by reader.

**Step 2 — Agitation:** Inject emotion. Tap anger, resentment, fear, embarrassment, guilt. Paint consequences vividly. Whip into fervor.

**Step 3 — Solution Unveiling:** Position your offer as answer. Reader mentally ready ("I've got to do something!").

**Example Structure:**
- PROBLEM: "Small business owners are the government's #1 tax target. New forms arrive monthly. Tax reform hits hardest."
- AGITATION: "You work hard. Employees steal time. Vendors steal goods. And now the government steals profit through taxes you could legally avoid."
- SOLUTION: "This tax-strategies course shows small business owners the secrets normally only 'big guys' use. You'll save thousands annually."

## Visualization Pattern (For Copywriter)

**Method:**
1. Picture reader as living, breathing person
2. Visualize their day from start: commute, email, mail handling
3. Picture when/where they read your letter
4. Mentally note their preoccupations, worries, wishes
5. Anticipate their objections and thoughts
6. Write to this mental image as if to one person

**Result:** Copy anticipates reader's reactions. Addresses concerns before reader thinks of them. Increases persuasiveness dramatically.

**Tool:** Dr. Maxwell Maltz's "Theater in Your Mind" visualization technique from Psycho-Cybernetics.

## Envelope/Physical Letter Patterns

### Pattern: Sneak-Up (Highest Delivery + Open Rate)
**Components:**
- Plain white envelope
- No business name on front
- Person's name only (no title)
- Individual or ink-jet addressed
- Live stamp (not meter)

**Result:** 98% delivery + open rate. Looks like personal letter, not junk mail.

---

### Pattern: Intimidating Official Design
**Components:**
- Very official-looking design (official letter more than express-mail look)
- "Audited Delivery" or "Important Documents" imprint
- Gold seal or special ink treatment

**Result:** Postal workers treat as legitimate mail. Recipients take seriously.

---

### Pattern: Inside Delivery on Envelope Promise
**Structure:** Envelope makes promise → inside content must fulfill it immediately.

**Example:**
- Envelope says "Doctor's Name Return Address"
- Inside has small lift letter from doctor endorsing offer
- Or: Envelope says "Personal" → inside has personal message/story, not generic sales pitch

**Trade-offs:** Builds credibility when promise fulfilled; destroys credibility when broken; make it honest.
