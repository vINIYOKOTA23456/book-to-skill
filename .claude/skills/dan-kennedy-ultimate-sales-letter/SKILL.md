# Dan Kennedy: The Ultimate Sales Letter

Master the 28-step system for writing sales letters that sell. Kennedy's framework remains the gold standard in direct-response copywriting.

---

## The 28-Step System Overview

Kennedy's system is sequential and comprehensive. Every step builds on the previous one. Follow all 28 — skipping steps creates weak letters.

**Steps 1-3 (Before Writing):** Get into customer psychology, get into the offer details, acknowledge flaws openly  
**Steps 4-6 (Physical Delivery):** Get delivered, get looked at, get read  
**Steps 7-28 (Copy Execution):** Beat price objections, use techniques, draft, rewrite, enhance, mail

## Core Framework: The 10 Smart Market Diagnosis Questions

Before writing anything, answer these 10 questions about your customer/prospect:

1. **What keeps them awake at night?** (anxiety, indigestion, staring at ceiling)
2. **What are they afraid of?** (loss, failure, embarrassment)
3. **What are they angry about?** (unfairness, being wronged, unmet needs)
4. **What are their top three daily frustrations?** (time, money, relationships)
5. **What trends are occurring in their business/life?** (shifts, threats, opportunities)
6. **What do they secretly, ardently desire most?** (the hidden want)
7. **Is there a built-in bias to how they make decisions?** (analytical, emotional, peer-driven)
8. **Do they have their own language/jargon?** (speak their vernacular)
9. **Who else is selling them something similar, and how?** (competition map)
10. **Who has tried and failed to sell them before?** (learn from failures)

**Rule:** Write directly to this profile. Use "you/your" as if speaking to one specific person. Visualization is key — see them, their day, their concerns, their wins.

## The Three Master Formulas (Problem-Solution Framework Ranked by Effectiveness)

### Formula #1: Problem-Agitation-Solution (Most Reliable)

**Step 1 — Define the Problem:** State it clearly, factually. May be known or unknown by reader. Get agreement.

**Step 2 — Agitate:** Inject emotion. Stir up anger, resentment, fear, embarrassment. Make it larger than life. Paint the consequences vividly. Whip them into a fervor.

**Step 3 — Unveil the Solution:** Position your offer as the answer. Present benefits. Reader is now mentally ready (hands wringing, pacing, saying "I've got to do something!").

**Why it works:** People act to avoid pain more than to get gain. PAS taps loss aversion psychology.

**Usage:** Works for any industry. Best for complex objections or high-stakes decisions.

---

### Formula #2: Fortune Telling (Prediction + Fascination)

Position yourself as the expert who predicts the future. Tap reader's fascination with "what's coming."

**Structure:** What lies ahead in your industry? For your customers? What danger should they protect against now? What opportunity is budding that they should attach to?

**Psychology:** People are fascinated by predictive content. Use fear + opportunity + urgency of timing.

**Examples:** Y2K products, stock market predictions, industry trend warnings.

**Duration of power:** Time-tested, still works decades later with only language updates.

---

### Formula #3: Winners & Losers (Contrast + Fear of Missing Out)

Compare two groups: winners (who adopt your solution) vs. losers (who don't).

**Structure:** Create stark contrast. "Follow path A or end up in group B." The comparison could be statistical (5% successful, 95% broke), anecdotal (two neighbors, two lawn outcomes), or professional (two competitors, one thrives).

**Psychology:** Loss aversion + social proof. Readers want to be on the winning side.

**Power:** Works because it's universal. People understand immediately. Creates fear of failure.

## The 6 Headline Structures That Work (Fill-in-the-Blank)

Headlines capture >50% of your letter's power. Use these proven structures:

1. **"They Didn't Think I Could __, But I Did"** — Underdog appeal. Overcome-odds narrative.  
   *Examples:* "They Laughed When I Sat Down at the Piano—But Not When I Started to Play!"

2. **"Who Else Wants __?"** — Implication that others know a secret you don't. Social proof.  
   *Examples:* "Who Else Wants a Screen-Star Figure?" / "Who Else Needs an Extra Hour Every Day?"

3. **"How __ Made Me __"** — First-person story with dramatic difference.  
   *Examples:* "How a 'Fool Stunt' Made Me a Star Salesman" / "How I Retired at 40 with Guaranteed Income"

4. **"Are You __?"** — Question headline. Challenges, provokes, arouses curiosity.  
   *Examples:* "Are You Ashamed of Smells in Your House?" / "Are You Prepared for the Japanese Invasion of Your Industry?"

5. **"How To __"** — Straightforward, powerful. "How to" = two most powerful words.  
   *Examples:* "How to Collect Social Security at Any Age" / "How to Improve Telemarketers' Productivity for $19.95"  
   *Variation:* Add a flag for targeting: "For Busy Doctors: How to Educate New Patients in Half the Time"

6. **"Give Me __ and I'll __"** — Telegraphs your offer. Promise simplicity.  
   *Examples:* "Give Me 5 Days and I'll Give You a Magnetic Personality" / "Give Me Just 1 Hour a Day and I'll Have You Speaking French in One Month"

**Advanced Headlines:** "Secrets of __" (power word), "WARNING: __" (attention + problem focus), "__Ways to __" (specific number), "If You Are __, You Can __" (segmentation + benefit)

**Rule:** Interrogative (question), specific numbers, and problem-solution headlines consistently outperform generic benefits.

## Price Psychology: 7 Proven Minimizers

Price is rarely THE deciding factor, but mishandled, it kills sales before they start.

### 1. Compare Apples to Oranges
Don't compare product-to-product at same price level. Compare to higher-priced alternative.  
*Example:* Cassette course ($95) vs. seminar attendance ($195 + travel + time).

### 2. Sell Bulk
People equate value with bulk. List weight, size, quantity of contents.  
*Example:* "Five-foot shelf of books" / "Over 13 pounds of books, nearly $20 just to ship"

### 3. Discuss the Price Paid to Develop It
Emphasize research cost, expert time, development rigor. Makes price justify itself.  
*Example:* "Eight top robotics engineers, six months lab testing, $1M+ research, 15,000 test repetitions"

### 4. Make the Parts Worth More Than the Whole
Break down every component, feature, bonus. Add up individual values so total >> price.  
*Example:* 6 seminars ($1,200 value) + 24-month kit ($10,000 value) + tape club ($1,000 value) = $12,200 total value, but price only $3,895.

### 5. Conceal the Price
State as monthly installments rather than total. Hide the full number.  
*Example:* "Just three small monthly installments of $11.95" sounds smaller than "$35.85"

### 6. Payment Plans
Use installments, financing, subscription model. Spreads pain of payment.

### 7. Hidden Benefit Positioning
Sell the dream outcome, not the product. Emphasize what they'll gain in lifestyle/time/status.  
*Example:* "Puts recruiting on auto-pilot so you can go play golf"

## The Physical Letter: Getting Delivered and Opened

**The Problem:** 10-30% (or more) of bulk mail never reaches recipients. Postal workers deliberately destroy "junk mail."

**Envelope Strategy Options:**
- **Sneak-Up Approach:** Plain white, no business name, just person's name return address, individual ink-jet addressing, live stamp → 98% delivery + open rate
- **Intimidating Imprints:** "Audited Delivery," "Important Documents," "The Information You Requested"
- **Official-Looking Design:** Very official appearance works better than fake express envelopes (now tired)
- **First Class vs. Bulk:** First class with live stamp trumps bulk mail (lower cost is false economy if 20% gets trashed)
- **Hand-Addressed or Ink-Jet:** Gummed labels scream "junk mail"

**Opening Strategy:** Fulfill the envelope promise inside. If envelope says "Personal," letter must be personal. If says "Doctor's Name," enclose small lift note from that doctor.

## First Paragraph Law: Earn Welcome Guest Status

Reader sorts mail over wastebasket. You have one moment to go from "annoying pest" to "welcome guest."

**How:** Say something of genuine importance and value to reader. Not gimmicks. Not clever tricks. Genuine relevance.

**Example that fails:** "WARNING: This letter is impregnated with hazardous chemical that explodes if discarded." (Cute, tossed unread)

**Example that works:** State a problem they have, or a fact they don't know but care about deeply.

**Format:** Use a headline (can be above salutation, in Johnson box, or between salutation and body).

**First Paragraph Rule:** Follow headline's promise immediately. Short punchy sentences. Deliver the reading reward promised in headline. If you can skip the first paragraph and start with paragraph two, your first paragraph failed.

## Copy Techniques That Work Universally

### Technique #1: Intimidation (Positioning)

Take a psychological position of confidence. Don't need the sale → easier to close.

- **Limited Number Available:** "If response received after supply exhausted, check will be returned uncashed" = intimidating
- **Most Will Buy:** Bandwagon effect. "Thousands joined in 30 days. We've added extra staff to handle calls."
- **You Will Buy Only If:** Challenge reader's ego/pride. "It takes a very special individual to appreciate this value"

### Technique #2: Testimonials (Proof)

Powerful proof source. Most effective when:
- From person like the reader (same profession, age, background)
- Geographically close (same city/region)
- Specific (names, numbers, results, not generic praise)
- About specific outcome, not general satisfaction

### Technique #3: Damaging Admission

Acknowledge flaws in your offer openly. Enhances credibility.  
*Example:* "If you want white-glove service in fancy dining, we're not your place. But if you want real Italian food at real prices with red checkered tablecloths, you'll love us."

### Technique #4: Case Histories & Slice-of-Life Stories

Make intangible benefits tangible. Show real people getting real results. "Before/after" format works.

### Technique #5: Specificity Over Generality

Never say "quality products" or "great results." Say "$3,847 saved in first year" or "improved tennis performance by 15%." Numbers beat adjectives.

### Technique #6: The Second Person Singular

Use "you" not "people." Write as if to one person. Creates intimacy and connection.

## Copywriting Anti-Patterns (What Fails)

- ❌ Vague generalities ("Quality is our priority")
- ❌ Multiple competing appeals (confuses reader)
- ❌ Features not translated to benefits (focus on what it does, not what it is)
- ❌ Facts without emotional setup (cold, unconvincing)
- ❌ Emotion without factual support (unbelievable)
- ❌ Unclear call to action (reader doesn't know what to do)
- ❌ Assuming reader has background knowledge (spell it out)
- ❌ Relying on reader to infer what you want (state it explicitly)

## The Professional Copywriter's Mindset

### Principle #1: Sales First, Compliments Never
Copy exists to produce sales and revenue, not client approval. A campaign is good only when proven by orders and money.

### Principle #2: Know What to Avoid
Red flags that repeatedly fail:
- Campaigns without immediate results rarely succeed over time
- Don't rely on "compound effect" (multiple weak exposures accumulating)
- Weak ads mailed repeatedly = zero multiplied by anything = zero
- Overly complex copy loses attention regardless of content quality

### Principle #3: Develop Humility Through Doubt
Question everything:
- Is my headline convincing enough to change minds?
- Is my copy persuasive enough to create demand?
- Can my copy overcome weak sales support?
- Can I neutralize price objections?
- Would I buy based on this copy?

## Copy Length Decision Matrix

**Is inherent appeal strong?** (Product naturally interesting, benefits obvious)
- YES → Short copy possible (50-200 words)
- NO → Longer copy needed (1,000+ words)

**Is price high?** ($500+)
- YES → Longer copy (need extensive proof)
- NO → Shorter copy often sufficient

**Is decision complex?** (Multiple factors, high involvement)
- YES → Longer copy (education needed)
- NO → Shorter copy possible

**Can you hold interest?** (Do you have multiple strong points?)
- YES → Go as long as needed
- NO → Cut ruthlessly to strongest point

**Rule:** "As long as needed, never longer." Length is determined by how much sales work needs doing in the letter. Interest is the variable that matters.

## Visualization Technique (Secret Weapon)

Use visualization to become one with your prospect.

**Method:** Picture them as a living, breathing, thinking person. Visualize:
- Their day's experience from start
- How they get their mail (presorted? hand-delivered?)
- Where/when they read it
- What else they're thinking about
- What they worry about, wish for, enjoy
- Their fears and secret desires

**Purpose:** Anticipate their thoughts and reactions. If you lack this info, research it. Go to libraries, attend their meetings, eavesdrop on industry conversations. Get that understanding before writing.
