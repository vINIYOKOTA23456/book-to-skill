# Glossary: Dan Kennedy Sales Letter Terms

**10 Smart Market Diagnosis Questions** — Kennedy's pre-writing checklist: 10 questions about customer psychology, fears, desires, frustrations, trends, biases, and previous failed attempts to reach them. (Step 1)

**Agitation (The Second Force)** — Second step of Problem-Agitation-Solution formula; injecting emotion into the problem, stirring up fear/anger/resentment, making the problem larger than life. (Formula #1)

**Annoying Pest to Welcome Guest** — Kennedy's principle: reader starts by viewing your letter as unwanted junk; elevation to "welcome guest" occurs only when letter says something of genuine importance and value. (Step 6)

**AIDA Formula** — Person-to-person selling formula: Attention → Interest → Desire → Action. Applies directly to sales letter sequence.

**Bandwagon Effect** — Intimidation technique; creating perception that huge trend has developed and everyone is getting involved, making non-participation seem foolish.

**Damaging Admission** — Copywriting technique where writer acknowledges product/service flaws openly, enhancing credibility and addressing objections preemptively. (Step 3)

**Direct-Response Copywriting** — Writing designed to move reader to immediate action (order, call, reply), not just build brand awareness.

**Fill-in-the-Blank Headlines** — Kennedy's 6+ proven headline templates that consistently work across industries (They Didn't Think..., Who Else Wants..., How Made Me..., Are You..., How To..., Give Me...).

**First Paragraph Law** — Critical principle: first paragraph determines if reader continues; must follow headline promise, use short punchy sentences, start with strongest benefit, deliver reading reward immediately.

**Fortune Telling Formula** — Master formula #2; positioning yourself as predictive expert, tapping reader fascination with future outcomes, using prediction + fear + opportunity + urgency. (Formula #2)

**Hidden Benefit** — Advanced copywriting strategy; emphasizing non-obvious benefit that is profoundly important to customer, not the first/obvious benefit. Example: "Puts recruiting on auto-pilot so you can go play golf" (the golf is the hidden benefit).

**Intimidation (Positioning)** — Copywriting technique where writer takes position of not needing the sale, creating confidence that unconsciously reassures reader; includes limited quantity, bandwagon, and ego-challenge variants.

**Johnson Box** — Device in sales letters (box with decorative borders) containing headline or key offer statement, placed between salutation and body copy to draw attention.

**Letter vs. Advertisement** — Kennedy's distinction: traditional letters have no headline, but successful sales letters DO use headlines (above salutation, in Johnson box, or between salutation and body).

**Lift Letter** — Small enclosure (typically scratchpad-sized) from recognized expert endorsing main offer, placed in envelope to increase open rate and credibility.

**Problem-Agitation-Solution Formula** — Master formula #1; most reliable copywriting framework with three-step structure: define problem clearly, agitate emotions about problem, unveil solution positioned as answer. (Formula #1)

**Prospect Visualization** — Kennedy's secret technique; mentally seeing prospect as living person—their day, their mail-handling habits, their concerns, fears, desires—to anticipate reactions and write persuasively.

**Sneak-Up Approach** — Envelope strategy using plain white envelope, no business name, person's name return address, individual ink-jet addressing, live stamp = highest delivery + open rates (98%).

**Swipe File** — Reference collection of ads, mailings, sales letters, and copy samples organized by category; used to recycle ideas, themes, words, and phrases (not copied verbatim). Essential tool for copywriters.

**Telemarketer Analogy** — Kennedy compares sales letter to door-to-door salesperson with foot in door; headline has same length of time and opportunity to convert pest to guest and create interest.

**Testimonial Effectiveness Ranking** — Strongest when: (1) from person like reader, (2) geographically close, (3) specific results with numbers/names, (4) about specific outcome not general satisfaction.

**The 28-Step System** — Kennedy's comprehensive sequential sales-letter writing process spanning from customer research through final mail drop; every step builds on previous; skipping steps weakens letter.

**Three Master Formulas** — Kennedy's three most reliable copywriting structures: Problem-Agitation-Solution (most reliable), Fortune Telling (prediction + fascination), Winners & Losers (contrast + loss aversion).

**Two-Force Rule** — Fundamental copywriting principle: emotion alone creates desire without conviction; facts alone create doubt without desire; together they create sale. (Problem + Solution need both.)

**Visualization Technique** — Advanced copywriter method using Dr. Maxwell Maltz's psycho-cybernetics to mentally rehearse letter recipient's day, anticipate thoughts/reactions, become "one with" the prospect.

**Winners & Losers Formula** — Master formula #3; contrasting two groups (winners who adopt solution vs. losers who don't) creating fear of missing out and loss aversion motivation. (Formula #3)

**You (Second Person Singular)** — Fundamental principle: write to individual reader not "people"; use "you/your" throughout to create intimacy; Kennedy rule—personalization beats generic.

**Postal Non-Delivery Problem** — Real industry issue: 10-30%+ of properly addressed third-class mail never reaches recipients; postal workers deliberately destroy perceived "junk mail"; requires envelope strategy to overcome.

**Prestige Words** — Words that convey exclusivity/status in executive mailings: alternative, association, attractive, charter, exceptional, exclusive, individual, membership, ownership, preferred, prominent, select, superior, uncompromising, worthwhile, yield.

**Price Comparison Strategy** — Apples-to-oranges technique; don't compare product-to-product at equivalent prices; instead compare to higher-priced alternative (cassette vs. seminar, subscription vs. books).

**Price Minimizers (7 Types)** — Kennedy's methods for neutralizing price objections: compare to higher alternative, emphasize bulk, discuss development cost, break down component values, conceal in installments, use payment plans, emphasize hidden benefits.

**Reader Portrait** — Mental model of prospect built from demographics, education, daily concerns, fears, beliefs, superstitions, decision biases, language/jargon, competitive context; write directly to this portrait.

**Specificity Principle** — Copywriting rule: never say "quality" or "great results"; always quantify with numbers, percentages, time saved, money earned; "$3,847 saved annually" beats "significant savings."

**Underdog Appeal** — Headline formula "They Didn't Think I Could..." taps natural human tendency to root for underdog; fascinates with stories of overcoming obstacles and others' ridicule.
