# Cheatsheet: Quick Reference & Decision Rules

## Copywriting Process at a Glance

**Pre-Writing:**
1. Research and immerse (10 Smart Questions about audience, product, market)
2. Identify dominant emotion and unique concept
3. Build reader profile (age, concerns, desires, language)

**Incubation:**
4. Saturate your mind with information
5. Step away and allow subconscious processing
6. Wait for illumination (the breakthrough idea)

**Writing:**
7. Draft without perfectionism (get it down)
8. Create slippery slide (each sentence pulls to next)
9. Write strong first sentence (short, simple, interesting)
10. Position concept, not product
11. Build buying environment (credibility, safety, resonance)

**Revision:**
12. Rewrite for emotional resonance
13. Rewrite for clarity and flow
14. Add proof (testimonials, data, case studies)
15. Strengthen call to action
16. Challenge assumed constraints
17. Edit out non-essentials

---

## The First Sentence Checklist

Before finalizing your first sentence, verify:

- [ ] Is it short? (15 words or fewer ideally)
- [ ] Is it simple? (No jargon, no complex ideas)
- [ ] Is it interesting? (Creates curiosity or immediate relevance)
- [ ] Does it address the reader's dominant concern?
- [ ] Does it compel reading the second sentence?
- [ ] Does it set the tone for the slippery slide?
- [ ] Can I remove it without damaging the message? (If yes, it's not strong enough)
- [ ] Does it tap into the dominant emotion I identified?

## Slippery Slide Decision Tree

```
START: Is your reader engaged and sliding through your copy?

├─ YES
│  └─ STOP → Your flow is working. Measure results.
│
└─ NO → What's breaking the slide?
   │
   ├─ Sentences too long?
   │  └─ FIX: Break into shorter sentences. Aim for 8-12 words average.
   │
   ├─ Ideas too complex?
   │  └─ FIX: Simplify. One idea per sentence. Move complexity to later sentences.
   │
   ├─ Topic shifts suddenly?
   │  └─ FIX: Create bridge sentences. Gradual transitions. Show how ideas connect.
   │
   ├─ Reader can skip paragraphs without losing meaning?
   │  └─ FIX: Make each sentence essential. Remove padding. Create forward momentum.
   │
   └─ Copy feels emotionally cold?
      └─ FIX: Inject emotional language. Use vivid scenarios. Tap identified emotion throughout.
```

## Buying Environment Checklist

Create these elements before readers will feel comfortable purchasing:

**Credibility:**
- [ ] What makes you qualified to speak on this?
- [ ] What's your track record?
- [ ] What do satisfied customers say?
- [ ] Do you address the reader's likely skepticism?

**Safety:**
- [ ] What fears might the reader have? (Financial risk? Embarrassment? Failure?)
- [ ] Have you explicitly addressed each fear?
- [ ] Do you offer a guarantee or risk reversal?
- [ ] Is the mechanism simple and clear?

**Resonance:**
- [ ] Does the copy show deep understanding of the reader's world?
- [ ] Does the reader nod in recognition at multiple points?
- [ ] Do you use the reader's language and terminology?
- [ ] Are you speaking to their actual concerns or assumed concerns?

**Inevitability:**
- [ ] Does the copy build logically to a conclusion?
- [ ] Does the reader feel buying is the obvious next step?
- [ ] Have you presented alternatives and shown why yours is superior?
- [ ] Does every section reinforce the primary message?

**Ease:**
- [ ] Is the next step crystal clear?
- [ ] Can the reader complete the action in under 2 minutes?
- [ ] Are there multiple response options (phone, web, mail)?
- [ ] Do you provide exact instructions?

## Headline Effectiveness Matrix

| Headline Type | Curiosity | Credibility | Emotional Power | Best For | Avoid If |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Specificity ("How to ___ in 7 Days") | ⭐⭐ | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ | ⭐⭐ | Clear benefits, courses, how-tos | Product is intangible or complex |
| Benefit ("Imagine ___") | ⭐⭐⭐ | ⭐⭐ | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ | Emotional products, lifestyle | Need rational credibility |
| Question ("Are You ___?") | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ | ⭐ | ⭐⭐⭐ | Engagement, problem-solution | Audience doesn't identify with question |
| Contrast ("From ___ to ___") | ⭐⭐⭐ | ⭐⭐⭐ | ⭐⭐⭐ | Transformation, before/after | Subtle changes or incremental benefits |
| Data/Numbers ("___ Ways to ___") | ⭐⭐⭐ | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ | ⭐⭐ | Lists, frameworks, processes | Simple single-benefit offers |
| Direct Curiosity ("I Almost ___") | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ | ⭐ | ⭐⭐⭐ | Engagement and pull-through | Trust is already an issue |

**Decision Rule:** Use "How to," "Imagine," or contrast headlines when uncertain. These outperform generic benefits across industries.

## Reader Psychology Quick Reference

| Psychological Principle | How It Works | Copy Application |
|---|---|---|
| **Emotion Over Logic** | Purchases driven by feeling; logic justifies | Appeal to emotion first, then provide logical support |
| **Visualization** | Readers mentally rehearse using the product | Use vivid, sensory language; paint scenarios |
| **Resonance** | Reader agreement and recognition = belief | Show deep understanding of reader's world |
| **Social Proof** | Others want it → I should too | Use testimonials, data, "most will buy" positioning |
| **Loss Aversion** | Fear of loss > desire for gain | Emphasize what they lose if they don't act |
| **Scarcity** | Limited supply = higher value | Limited spots, time limits, inventory limits |
| **Authority** | Expert says it → must be true | Use expert endorsements, credentials, experience |
| **Reciprocity** | Free gift = obligation to respond | Offer bonus, sample, trial, guarantee |

## Proof Strategy Selection Matrix

| Situation | Best Proof Type | Why It Works | Implementation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reader skeptical | Data + Testimonial | Numbers convince rationally; testimonials convince emotionally | "97% reported results (data). John says: 'Life-changing.'" |
| Product is intangible | Before/After case study | Makes abstract benefit concrete and visualizable | Show real person: before (struggling), after (thriving) |
| Price is high ($500+) | Component breakdown + Guarantee | Multiple proofs needed to justify investment | List value of each component; offer money-back guarantee |
| Reader distrusts marketing | Expert endorsement + Damaging admission | Authority + honesty = credibility | "Dr. X endorses this. Note: It's not for everyone." |
| Competitor alternative exists | Apples-to-oranges comparison | Shows your unique value clearly | "Competitor charges $5K. We deliver same value for $1.5K." |
| Audience is pragmatic/analytical | Statistical data + ROI calculation | Pragmatic buyers want numbers and clear ROI | "Average client sees $50K ROI in year 1" |
| Audience is emotional/aspirational | Testimonials + visualization scenarios | Emotional buyers respond to relatability and dreams | "Sarah, a mom like you, now has her evenings back." |

## Copy Length Decision Matrix

| Factor | Decision |
|---|---|
| **Strong inherent appeal?** YES | Short copy possible (50-200 words) |
| **Strong inherent appeal?** NO | Long copy needed (1,000+ words) |
| **Price $500+?** YES | Long copy (need extensive proof) |
| **Price $500+?** NO | Medium/short often sufficient |
| **Decision complex?** YES | Long copy (education needed) |
| **Decision complex?** NO | Short copy possible |
| **Can hold reader interest?** YES | As long as needed |
| **Can hold reader interest?** NO | Cut ruthlessly to strongest point |
| **Multiple proof points available?** YES | Can be longer |
| **Multiple proof points available?** NO | Keep concise |

**Final Rule:** "As long as needed, never longer." Length = amount of sales work required. Interest and relevance determine optimal length.

## The 10 Audience Research Questions (Before Writing)

| # | Question | Why It Matters | Application |
|---|----------|---|---|
| 1 | What keeps them awake at night? | Anxiety reveals core concerns | Address fears head-on in opening |
| 2 | What are they afraid of? | Fear drives behavior more than desire | Tap fear to create urgency |
| 3 | What are they angry about? | Anger channels into motivation | Channel frustration into action |
| 4 | What are their top frustrations? | Daily pain points reveal needs | Position product as pain reliever |
| 5 | What trends affect their industry? | Context shapes decision-making | Position as response to trend |
| 6 | What do they secretly desire? | Hidden wants trump obvious wants | Emphasize aspirational benefit |
| 7 | How do they make decisions? | Analytical, emotional, or peer-driven? | Match copy style to decision bias |
| 8 | What's their language/jargon? | Insiders speak insider language | Use their terminology, not yours |
| 9 | Who else is selling similarly? | Competition reveals positioning gaps | Differentiate vs. competition |
| 10 | Who tried and failed before? | Previous failures teach lessons | Address specific objections from failures |

## Copy Fails: Diagnostic Checklist

If your copy isn't working, check these red flags:

- ❌ **Reads like feature list, not benefits** — Product focused, not reader focused
- ❌ **No emotional appeal, just facts** — Logic without emotion fails to persuade
- ❌ **Reader doesn't know what to do** — Call to action is vague or missing
- ❌ **Longer than needed to make sale** — Padding instead of punch
- ❌ **Focus on product, not reader benefit** — Wrong angle entirely
- ❌ **No proof/support for claims** — Reader skepticism not addressed
- ❌ **Vague benefits or generic language** — Could apply to any product
- ❌ **Reader has multiple interpretations** — Ambiguous messaging
- ❌ **First paragraph doesn't follow headline** — Broken promise
- ❌ **Complex sentences in opening** — Loses busy reader immediately
- ❌ **Starts with features instead of benefits** — Wrong angle
- ❌ **Delayed reward for reading** — Doesn't deliver promised value early
- ❌ **No emotional connection** — Feels cold and corporate
- ❌ **First paragraph too long** — More than 3-4 sentences
- ❌ **Copy can skip paragraphs without losing meaning** — Padding not moving reader forward

## Copy Works: Success Checklist

When your copy is working, you'll see:

- ✅ **Reader can't skip first paragraph** — Too compelling to ignore
- ✅ **Emotional connection is immediate** — Reader feels understood
- ✅ **Every major claim has proof** — No naked assertions
- ✅ **Call to action is impossible to miss** — Crystal clear next step
- ✅ **Reader visualizes using product** — Tangible, not abstract
- ✅ **Objections addressed before reader thinks them** — Anticipated concerns handled
- ✅ **Price feels justified by value** — Alignment between benefits and cost
- ✅ **Reader feels urgency to act NOW** — Not "someday"
- ✅ **Copy speaks in reader's language** — Uses their terminology
- ✅ **Emotional peaks distributed throughout** — Maintains engagement
- ✅ **Proof is specific, not generic** — Named sources, numbered results
- ✅ **Each section reinforces core message** — No mixed signals
- ✅ **Tone matches audience** — Formal for business, conversational for consumer

## Incubation Process Quick Reference

**Phase 1: Immersion (1-2 days)**
- Saturate mind with research
- Read customer feedback, complaints, objections
- Study competitor ads closely
- Interview customers if possible
- Identify unique angles and insights

**Phase 2: Incubation (1-3 days)**
- STOP conscious work on the problem
- Engage in other activities (walk, exercise, sleep, hobbies)
- Allow subconscious to process information
- Don't force it; let it happen naturally

**Phase 3: Illumination (Unpredictable)**
- The breakthrough appears (often unexpectedly)
- Usually during rest, shower, driving, or sleep
- Idea comes fully formed, not piecemeal
- Excitement signals authenticity of insight

**Phase 4: Implementation (Immediate)**
- Capture idea before it fades
- Write it down exactly as it comes
- Don't censor; don't perfect
- Implementation while fresh > polishing

## Editing Checklist (After First Draft)

**Emotional Resonance Pass:**
- [ ] Mark every sentence with emotional impact
- [ ] Are strongest emotional moments front-loaded?
- [ ] Do emotions build throughout?
- [ ] Any dry, logical patches that need color?
- [ ] Core emotion reinforced in every section?

**Slippery Slide Pass:**
- [ ] Does each sentence compel the next?
- [ ] Any sentences that feel like stopping points?
- [ ] Opening sentences short enough? (15 words or fewer)
- [ ] Flow interrupted by complex ideas or topic shifts?
- [ ] Patterns and rhythm working?

**Benefit Focus Pass:**
- [ ] Are benefits (not features) leading?
- [ ] Is reader benefit clear in every section?
- [ ] Product mentions secondary to benefit?
- [ ] Visualization-triggering language throughout?

**Proof Pass:**
- [ ] Every major claim supported by proof?
- [ ] Proof is specific (named, numbered, detailed)?
- [ ] Proof placed where reader doubt exists?
- [ ] Emotional proof (testimonials) + rational proof (data)?

**Call to Action Pass:**
- [ ] Is next step crystal clear?
- [ ] Can reader complete action in under 2 minutes?
- [ ] Multiple response options provided?
- [ ] Exact instructions given?
- [ ] Urgency elements present?

## Anti-Patterns (Never Do This)

- ❌ Start with vague generality ("Quality is our priority")
- ❌ Use clever wordplay instead of clarity
- ❌ Lead with product features instead of benefits
- ❌ Try multiple competing appeals (confuses reader)
- ❌ Assume reader has background knowledge (spell it out)
- ❌ Include facts without emotional setup (feels cold)
- ❌ Include emotion without factual support (feels unbelievable)
- ❌ Make call to action vague or implicit (reader confused)
- ❌ Use reader-distancing language ("people" instead of "you")
- ❌ Trust weak ad repeated equals strong effect (false economy)
- ❌ Hide key information behind flowery language
- ❌ Use technical jargon for consumer audience
- ❌ Make reader infer what you want (state it explicitly)
- ❌ Compromise clarity for cleverness
- ❌ Sacrifice first-sentence power for variety

## The Professional Copywriter's Mindset

**Principle 1: Sales First, Compliments Never**
Copy exists to produce sales and revenue, not client approval. Measurement proves effectiveness.

**Principle 2: Know What Kills Copy**
- Weak ads mailed repeatedly = zero multiplied
- No immediate results rarely improves over time
- Complexity kills engagement regardless of content quality
- Assumed constraints limit creative possibilities

**Principle 3: Harness Doubt for Excellence**
- Is my headline convincing enough to change minds?
- Is my copy persuasive enough to create demand?
- Can my copy overcome weak product or service?
- Can I neutralize price objections?
- Would I buy based on this copy?
- Is my strongest benefit leading?
- Have I addressed top 3 reader objections?
- Does first paragraph follow headline promise?

**Principle 4: Volume and Practice Matter**
- Sheer volume and experience improve skills dramatically
- First drafts are never best work
- Rewriting is where excellence emerges
- Study winning ads relentlessly
- Build swipe file of great copy
- Measure what works and why

## Quick Problem Diagnosis

**If readers aren't opening your mail/email:**
→ Check headline and envelope design. First job is to be opened.

**If readers open but don't read:**
→ Check first sentence and opening paragraph. They're deciding to continue or trash.

**If readers start but don't finish:**
→ Check slippery slide. Sentences too long? Ideas too complex? Flow broken?

**If readers finish but don't act:**
→ Check call to action. Is it clear? Compelling? Easy?

**If readers act but complain later:**
→ Check buying environment. Did copy oversell? Did you handle objections?

**If nothing seems to work:**
→ Step back. Challenge assumed constraints. Research your audience more deeply. Incubate. Try a different angle.
